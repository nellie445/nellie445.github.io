Hurricane Family Reunification Data Project

Structured missing-person information submitted by families during a hurricane response, transforming information collected through social media messages into organized records that could support an existing family-reunification effort.

Excel Data Collection Data Structuring Data Preparation Information Management Humanitarian Data

Project Overview

Following a hurricane disaster in Jamaica, an existing social-media page was being used as a point of contact for families seeking information about missing relatives. Family members submitted details through messages so that information could be organized and used by the wider reunification effort.

I worked remotely on the data side of that response. My role was not to build the reunification platform. I collected information from incoming social-media messages, identified the relevant details, and converted those submissions into a structured Excel dataset that could be used by the existing project.

The Data Problem

The source information arrived as human communication rather than as a ready-to-use dataset. Individual messages could contain names, aliases, ages, locations, family relationships, contact details, and additional context in different formats or with information missing.

The task was therefore to turn unstructured, inconsistent submissions into records with a repeatable structure while preserving the information needed for the reunification process.

My Contribution

  • Reviewed missing-person information submitted through social-media messages.
  • Extracted relevant details from free-form family submissions.
  • Created and maintained structured Excel records for the reunification effort.
  • Organized information into consistent fields so records could be used downstream.
  • Captured incomplete or unavailable information without inventing missing details.
  • Maintained status information as cases were recorded as found or not found.
  • Worked within a remote, time-sensitive disaster-response environment coordinated primarily through online communication.

Dataset Structure

The workbook I created used a 13-field structure to organize information consistently. Fields included first and last name, alias, age, last known location, parish, case status, current location, reporting person, social-media contact, phone number, image identifier, and additional information.

Example Workflow

Family message → Information extraction → Structured Excel record → Existing reunification effort

Working With Real-World Data

The records reflected the kinds of quality challenges that occur when data is collected during an emergency. Some submissions did not include an age or alias, locations were written at different levels of detail, and additional family context sometimes appeared in free-text fields. The spreadsheet provided a consistent structure for recording what was known while allowing unavailable information to remain explicitly unavailable.

This work required attention to both structure and context: the goal was not simply to populate cells, but to interpret incoming information accurately enough to convert it into usable records without changing the meaning of what families reported.

Privacy & Responsible Data Handling

The original dataset contains sensitive information about real people affected by a disaster, including names, locations, contact information, social-media identifiers, and family-provided details. For that reason, the source spreadsheet is not published as part of this portfolio.

Any examples used publicly for this project are intentionally described at the structural level rather than exposing the identities or personal information of the people represented in the original records.

What This Project Demonstrates

  • Transforming unstructured information into structured data.
  • Designing a practical spreadsheet structure around real information needs.
  • Data collection, organization, and preparation in a time-sensitive environment.
  • Working with incomplete and inconsistent real-world data.
  • Maintaining accuracy while interpreting free-form submissions.
  • Handling sensitive humanitarian information with appropriate privacy considerations.
  • Contributing data work to a larger system without overstating ownership of the platform itself.

Project Artifact

The original Excel workbook is retained privately because it contains personally identifiable information from affected individuals and their families. It was used to verify and document the structure and nature of my contribution to this case study.
